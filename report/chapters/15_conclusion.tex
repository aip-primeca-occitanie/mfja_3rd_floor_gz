\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

MFJA needed a reproducible environment that could connect its heterogeneous
third-floor robots to checked task execution.

In Phase~1, I designed the third-floor CAD and the furnishings of Rooms~314 and
315 in Autodesk Inventor. I then integrated the supplied rail cells and
attributed robot assets into Gazebo. In Phase~2, I implemented multi-shuttle
transport, versioned interfaces, visual and language grounding, symbolic
planning and a supervisor that verifies effects before reporting success.
Together, these elements form one chain from task-level English to verified
simulated effects, rather than separate demonstrations. Configuration,
automated tests, retained evidence and run instructions form the handover to
MFJA.

The declared evaluation succeeded in all 10 language cases and all 12 isolated
request-to-motion runs. It also succeeded in all 5 closed-loop fault cases. The
one-shot Final Test evaluated 4,680 targets and passed all 80 predeclared gates.
Segment classification was 99.9359\%; the stricter joint segment/position result
was 90.8974\% at $|\Delta s_{ratio}|\leq0.05$. The discrete facts supplied to
planning were therefore strong, but fine position along a rail remained the
clearest measured weakness. The retained evidence supports O1--O4 within the
stated scope. \Cref{tab:objective-results} maps each objective to its principal
evidence.

These claims apply to fixed simulated cameras, known shuttles, kinematic rail
motion and mostly one execution per scene. They do not establish physical
transfer, simultaneous six-robot motion, repeated-run reliability or certified
safety. The selected runtime is Gazebo-only and starts with command execution
disabled. The priority extensions are a genuinely shifted visual set, repeated
seeded campaigns, live-fault recovery and stronger concurrency tests.

For MFJA, the outcome is a reusable platform for teaching, integration and
controlled planning or perception comparisons. Working under platform
constraints required me to turn a broad request into testable contracts and
revise designs when weaknesses appeared. The main professional lesson for me
was learning to justify design choices with evidence, discuss them with my
supervisors and state clearly what the results do and do not demonstrate.
