\section{Building the English task interface}

\subsection{Local model selection and configuration}

Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct is the pretrained model selected for the interface
\citep{qwen25}. In my pipeline, it proposes structured task fields from English
requests. I ran the quantised GGUF file distributed in the official model card
\citep{qwen25gguf} locally on the CPU through \code{llama-cpp-python} v0.3.16,
a Python binding for \code{llama.cpp} \citep{llamacpppython,llamacpp}. I did not
fine-tune the model. The exact deterministic inference configuration and
artefact identity are indexed through \cref{app:reproducibility}.

Without fine-tuning, a versioned prompt constrains the model to one
\code{SemanticParseEnvelope} JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) object over permitted task fields,
identities, rails, stations and slots; it excludes PDDL, plan steps and device
or shuttle commands.

\subsection{From model output to a confirmed task}

A rule-based extractor records explicit facts (for example, shuttle colour,
rail or slot), Qwen proposes intent fields, and fixed-precedence fusion prevents
the proposal from overwriting explicit or confirmed context. The resulting
non-executable \code{TaskGoalDraft} is clarified, domain-validated and confirmed
before an immutable \code{TaskGoal} is issued.

For the indirect request ``Could you get a loaded shuttle over to the right
slot~4?'', the saved record shows the model supplying
\code{goal\_type=transport} and deterministic fusion/validation constraining
side, payload and destination. The ten-case evaluation is reported in
\cref{sec:language-contract-evidence}.

\section{Generating the Room~315 visual corpus}

\subsection{Paired-camera corpus and partitioning}

The generator enumerated all $2^8-1=255$ non-empty subsets of identities
L1--L4/R1--R4 and produced eight geometrically varied scenes per subset by
changing valid rail positions, payload states and switches. After each Gazebo
scene settled, it captured one synchronised image per overhead camera: 2,040
paired observations, or 4,080 RGB files. \Cref{fig:paired-camera-training}
shows one input.

One scene is one labelled paired-camera record; a visible-shuttle target is one
present identity with complete supervision and is the denominator used for the
per-shuttle metrics.

Gazebo identity, rail coordinate, payload state and projected box supply labels
stored separately from image references. Only paired RGB images are model
features; depth, controller state, point clouds and other oracle metadata remain
label-generation data.

\paragraph{Historical grouped protocol.}
The predecessor V3 experiment kept all variants of a presence configuration in
one partition. Its Validation selected epoch~14, and its Test was consumed once
after selection on 30 July 2026; it is therefore historical evidence, not the
current Final Test. Neither historical 256-scene partition directly enters V4,
although V3's Validation-selected backbone provides indirect initialisation
lineage. Current loaders denylist both historical partitions. Their split counts
are summarised in \cref{app:technical}, while the audit record is indexed through
\cref{app:reproducibility}.

\subsection{Current training, Validation and regression-set roles}

The current pool combines 1,528 legacy grouped-training scenes with 4,000
training-only hard cases. Weighted sampling emphasises rare rail--segment cells,
boundaries, switch zones and multi-blocker scenes. These cases were generated
before Final Test access rather than mined from its errors.

The separate hard-case Validation (512 scenes; 2,254 present targets) selected
V4, drove patience and supplied temperature/segment-threshold fitting. Only
after selection, the 256-scene post-selection regression set (1,085 targets)
checked the frozen candidate for degradation; it affected neither selection nor
calibration and is not Test evidence. Accessing the current Final Test remained prohibited
throughout development; it supplies the principal evidence below.

The labelled partitions were released only after the immutable attempt had
completed; this later publication changes availability, not their experimental
roles. \Cref{app:reproducibility} identifies the release.

\section{Designing and training the visual-state estimator}

The \emph{visual model} is the learned network; the \emph{visual-inference
runtime} adds preprocessing and observation validation; the \emph{closed-loop
runtime} further adds the task gateway, planner, supervisor and controllers.

\paragraph{Evolution of the visual pipeline.}
The early versions primarily evolved the data contract: V1 established
paired-camera inputs while keeping simulator-derived labels outside the model
input, and V2 added continuous along-rail localisation. V3 fixed the
representation to the eight known shuttle identities, resized both cameras to
$224\times224$, concatenated global features from a shared ImageNet-pretrained
ResNet-18 and predicted 200 values through one head. Review of the hard-case
behaviour exposed three mismatches between that contract and the known camera
geometry and topology. \Cref{tab:v3-v4-design-response} maps each diagnosis to
the corresponding V4 decision. V4 is the active visual model used by the
evaluated runtime; its results are in \cref{sec:visual-v4-results}.

\subsection{Rail-isolated 168-value learned contract}

Each $4{:}3$ image is resized to $320\times240$, ImageNet-normalised and
processed separately by shared ResNet-18 weights through \code{layer3}. Each
rail then has its own \code{layer4} and four identity-query heads; no camera
feature enters the other rail's late branch.

For each of L1--L4/R1--R4, the model outputs 14 segment scores, two loaded-state
scores, a four-value normalised box and one within-segment ratio: 21 values per
identity and 168 in total. Identity and the decoded topology then determine
side, segment length and metric position; these known quantities are not learned.
The output composition is summarised in \cref{app:technical}; its exact ordering
and diagnostic compatibility adapter remain in the model records indexed by
\cref{app:reproducibility}.

\subsection{Training and checkpoint selection}

The V1--V3 visual-state development cycle was trained on the KAIROS cluster
operated by CALMIP, using an NVIDIA GH200 GPU. The final V4 training,
fine-tuning and evaluation were performed locally on an NVIDIA RTX~3080 Laptop
GPU.

Verified predecessor weights initialised the shared stem through \code{layer3}
and each independent \code{layer4}; no joint head transferred, and rail-branch
parameters, batch-normalisation running statistics (BatchNorm buffers) and
query heads were optimised independently.

The newly initialised query heads were trained before progressively exposing
the deeper rail branches and shared stem. This reduced the chance that an
unstable new head would immediately disturb inherited features. Appearance
augmentation was allowed, whereas camera swaps and side-breaking flips were
forbidden because they would violate the rail contract. The exact optimisation,
sampling and evaluation controls are collected in
\cref{tab:v4-training-controls}.

Macro F1 averages the class-wise harmonic mean of precision and recall, giving
each segment class equal weight. The fixed Validation planning score was
\[
0.30F_{1,\mathrm{macro}}+0.15A_{\mathrm{min\,side}}
+0.20R_{\mathrm{worst\,cell}}+0.20A_{\mathrm{joint},0.05}
+0.075A_{\mathrm{switch}}+0.075A_{\mathrm{boundary}}.

All 12 epochs completed, the procedure selected epoch~11 and the patience
condition was not reached. The regression set could reject this frozen
candidate but could neither choose a checkpoint nor tune it; the Final Test
remained unavailable throughout.

Validation also fitted the segment-score calibration and online acceptance
floor. Loaded-state score remained uncalibrated. The runtime binds checkpoint,
architecture, preprocessing, camera order and topology fingerprint before
becoming ready. \Cref{tab:visual-threshold-map} separates these online checks
from labelled offline tolerances; \cref{app:reproducibility} binds the exact
selected-run artefacts.

\subsection{Evidence after the V3-to-V4 redesign}

The architectural diagnosis in the preceding sections was followed by a large
change on the retained hard-case Validation comparison
(\cref{tab:v3-v4-development-comparison}). The comparison is useful evidence of
the complete redesign and current training protocol, but it is not a controlled
ablation: resizing, branch isolation, output definition, data weighting and
training schedule changed together. It therefore does not prove how much of the
improvement came from any one change.

The comparison was development evidence: it justified carrying V4, rather than
V3, into the frozen qualification sequence, but it was not counted as Final-Test
performance. I stopped redesigning only after the selected epoch also met the
predeclared Validation and post-selection regression gates and its artefact,
preprocessing and topology identities were frozen. The independent
opposite-camera blank/shuffle check later changed own-side V4 outputs by zero at
recorded precision, supporting the intended rail isolation. Passing these gates
defined the decision to test the model; they were selection controls rather
than a guarantee of future predictions.

\subsection{Preregistered Final Test}

Before evaluation, I froze the checkpoint, scenario plan, evaluator, thresholds
and single-run policy. No Final-Test result could alter training, checkpoint
selection, calibration or thresholds. Here, \emph{preregistered} means that the
repository controls and hashes were fixed before any Test prediction, not that
the protocol was registered externally. The one-shot method, audit and results
are reported in \cref{sec:visual-v4-results}; their records are indexed through
\cref{app:reproducibility}.

The final AI layer emits only a confirmed task and scene record; deterministic
planning and supervision remain responsible for commands. The V3 failures
motivated the rail-isolated V4 design, whose frozen evaluation covers only the
known fleet and fixed Gazebo cameras (\cref{sec:visual-v4-results}).
