The internship was organised in two phases. The following sections separate the
supervisors' input, reused resources and my own work. The chronology, review
process and key architecture choices explain how the available resources became
the delivered system.

\section{Mission and work packages}

My initial assignment was to model Room~315 and its robot and rail equipment in
Gazebo. Gazebo simulates virtual robots, sensors and environments
\citep{gazeboharmonic}. Finishing the assignment ahead of schedule enabled a
second research work package without changing the internship dates.

The implemented work is organised into two phases:

\begin{description}
  \item[Phase 1---simulation foundation] Modelling Room~315 in Gazebo and
  creating reusable software that can start any selected combination of its
  simulated robots and rail equipment.
  \item[Phase 2---neuro-symbolic research] Using paired-camera images to
  estimate equipment state, converting English requests into checked task
  goals, then planning and checking each action before execution.
\end{description}

Here, I use \emph{neuro-symbolic} to mean learned image and language
interpretation combined with rule-based planning expressed in PDDL
\citep{pddl} and integrated through PlanSys2 \citep{plansys2}, followed by the
execution checks I implemented. Phase~1 supplied the simulation needed by
Phase~2; objectives O1--O4 in \cref{chap:introduction} remain the units used to
assess the results.

\section{Requirements and review process}

Requirements were recorded in operator options and guides, executable interface
rules and tests, and hash-identified dataset/model manifests. They covered
isolated robot communication, stable shuttle identity, sparse device updates,
fresh scene state, deterministic plan-to-command translation and observation of
every executed effect. Each property was checked at its implementation boundary;
the safeguards are detailed in
\cref{chap:transport,chap:interfaces,chap:ai-development}, the artefacts in
\cref{app:reproducibility}, and the objective assessment in
\cref{tab:objective-results}.

\section{Personal responsibility, collaboration and resources}

Within the shared repository, I designed the retained third-floor geometry in
Autodesk Inventor and the furniture and static contents of both furnished rooms
in the full-floor model. I exported and integrated them into Gazebo. For
Room~315, I created the dedicated room model, including its doors and interior
window. I then assembled the scene from the project-supplied rail-cell parts and
the attributed robot models.

My software work covered selectable robot launch, calibrated rail motion,
device behaviour and typed command and state interfaces. I also implemented the
task checks, PDDL/PlanSys2 integration and one-action-at-a-time supervision. I
created the paired-camera dataset and training process, tests, inventories,
operating guides and handover records.

Weekly reviews with Diane Bury and Olivier Stasse were the principal
coordination mechanism. I presented completed work, observed problems and
technical alternatives. After discussing their feedback, I implemented and
validated the agreed solution. Their institutional roles are shown in
\cref{fig:organisation}.

The technical chapters distinguish the first available host-supplied revision,
project and supervisor input, reused foundations, my contribution and the
validation I performed. The baseline does not imply an earlier repository state
that was not available for inspection.

No purchasing budget was assigned to me. Resource management instead concerned
the fixed schedule, access to the shared platform, graphics processing unit
(GPU) time for model training, simulation time, dataset and saved-model storage,
and the testing workload. I kept compute-intensive camera-processing and
language-model components optional and prioritised reusable configuration and
regression tests that detect whether changes break existing behaviour.

\subsection{Choosing PDDL/PlanSys2 over direct vision--language--action control}

Phase~2 first explored a limited VLA-style event-selection prototype. It used
planner-generated labels rather than human action demonstrations and produced no
valid closed-loop result; one run was excluded after detecting target leakage.
Room~315 is a finite domain, and the retained corpus labels scene state rather
than actions. I therefore chose inspectable PDDL/PlanSys2 plans and kept learning
at the checked language and visual-state boundary. The evidence and trade-off
are detailed once in \cref{sec:ai-planning-decision}.
