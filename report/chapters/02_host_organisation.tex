\chapter{Host Organisation and MFJA Robotics Platforms}
\label{chap:host}

The project took place in a shared university facility, so its users, equipment
and operating constraints shaped the technical design. Understanding
Universit\'e de Toulouse, MFJA, Room~315 and the division of responsibilities is
therefore necessary to explain the mission's educational, operational and
strategic value.

\section{Université de Toulouse}

The internship was hosted by Université de Toulouse, 118 route de Narbonne,
31062 Toulouse Cedex~9, and carried out at the MFJA site, 1 rue Tarfaya, 31400
Toulouse. The university has operated as an
experimental public institution since 1 January 2025
\citep[p.~5]{univtoulousefigures}. Its Nomenclature d'activités
française/Activité principale exercée (NAF/APE) code is 85.42Z, classified by
the French national statistics institute (INSEE) as \emph{higher education}
\citep{univtoulousesirene,insee8542z}. The same institutional document reports
about 39,000 students and 4,200 staff across the university
\citep[p.~5]{univtoulousefigures}; the internship itself took place in the
smaller MFJA environment.

\section{Maison de la Formation Jacqueline Auriol}

The MFJA is a shared engineering-education centre on the Toulouse Aerospace
innovation campus. Its activities combine initial and continuing education,
research, innovation and skills transfer. It brings together more than 1,500
students from 15 mechanical-engineering programmes; its official description
also identifies aeronautics and space partnerships and an automation and
robotics laboratory \citep{mfjaofficial}. Sharing the equipment among these
activities required configurable, multi-user software that remains maintainable
beyond one developer; this constraint shaped the package and launch architecture
in Chapters~5--6.

\section{The Room 315 robotics environment}

Room~315 contains two flexible transport cells: closed-loop rail systems whose
independently controlled pallet carriers are termed \emph{shuttles}. The cells
share one public topology---the canonical segment names and connectivity exposed
to ROS~2 and planning---but have distinct calibrated geometries, meaning that
each rail follows its own measured spatial path. Each cell has four numbered
shuttle slots.
Four industrial-robot workstations surround the two cells. In the configuration
used for this project, right-rail slots~1--2 are served by the Yaskawa HC10DT
workstation and slots~3--4 by the Stäubli TX2-60L workstation. On the left rail,
slots~1--2 are served by the Yaskawa HC10 workstation and slots~3--4 by the
KUKA KR6 R900 sixx workstation. Each circuit contains outer and inner branches,
four switching zones, indexing detectors and stoppers. Both rail networks are
directed, so a shuttle can traverse each segment only in its defined forward
direction. A switching zone diverts a shuttle between branches, an indexing
detector reports its alignment or presence, and a directional stopper holds or
releases an approaching shuttle. A shuttle may be empty or carry a payload.

The installation therefore combines mechanical modelling, industrial
automation, robot communication, shared-resource planning, computer vision and
task-level language in one platform.
\Cref{fig:room315-orientation} shows the simulated layout; Chapters~7--8 define
the rail topology, device semantics and supervisory constraints.

the internship.

\section{Economic and strategic value}

Because the host is a public university, turnover and export revenue do not
measure this mission's value. Its strategic document reports a 2024 global
budget of nearly EUR~474 million and EUR~114 million in purchases
\citep[p.~5]{univtoulousefigures}; these university-wide figures indicate
scale, not project return. The project is expected to create operational and
educational value through:

\begin{itemize}
  \setlength{\itemsep}{0.28\baselineskip}
  \item \textbf{reuse}: one model and launch framework avoids recreating an
  integration for each practical class or research project;
  \item \textbf{risk reduction}: simulation and supervision expose naming,
  topology and command errors before costly equipment is used;
  \item \textbf{asset visibility}: the digital environment supports remote
  preparation and makes available configurations easier to inspect;
  \item \textbf{training capacity}: repeatable scenarios create exercises and
  data without monopolising the physical cells;
  \item \textbf{maintainability}: typed interfaces, tests and documentation
  reduce dependence on knowledge held by one developer; and
  \item \textbf{technology transfer}: the platform may support demonstrations
  of current ROS~2, planning and AI methods while retaining explicit
  software-safety boundaries.
\end{itemize}

These constraints led me to prioritise configurable launch, stable interfaces
and documentation that could be reused after the internship.
