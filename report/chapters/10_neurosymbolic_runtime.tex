I translated each operating rule into predicates, action preconditions and
effects. I then tested valid cases and closely related invalid cases. The tests
covered both rails, slots, switches, stoppers, shuttles obstructing a route, and
interior and exterior paths. A valid state had to produce an allowed step.
Missing, stale, contradictory or impossible state had to produce no executable
command. After execution, new sensor and controller feedback had to match the
expected effect before replanning. These tests provide evidence for the covered
cases; they are not a mathematical proof that the domain contains no error.

\section{Why the executive uses one-step replanning}

A complete POPF sequence is inspectable, but later steps are predictions about a
state that has not yet been observed. Executing the whole sequence open-loop
would let an occupancy change, failed device update or incomplete arrival make
its remaining preconditions obsolete. The executive therefore translates and
checks only the first supported action, observes its effect and builds a fresh
problem before continuing. This costs additional planner calls, but it keeps the
next decision tied to accepted state rather than to an earlier symbolic forecast.

\paragraph{Engineering iteration for blocker staging.}
An early planning assumption treated several blocker destinations as if they
could be allocated from one unchanged observation. Dense configurations exposed
the flaw: moving the first blocker changes both occupancy and the capacity-safe
destination available to the next one. I changed the design to authorise one
relocation at a time, retain the required holding capacity, obtain a fresh
observation and then either relocate the next blocker or restore the normal
exterior route. Builder and regression cases cover both rails and full-rail
distributions; they establish the implemented bounded strategy, not a general
deadlock proof.

\section{Step 1---interpret and confirm the English task}

The CLI applies \cref{chap:ai-development}, resolves identities and colour
aliases, and clarifies missing fields before confirmation publishes
\code{TaskGoal}. Scene-dependent policies remain ungrounded: the gateway selects
the shuttle or admissible station slot from current state, while the planner
chooses actions.

\section{Step 2---read the current scene and ground the goal}

The hash-bound visual runtime checks schema, preprocessing, camera order,
freshness and confidence. Only the gateway can fuse accepted visual locations
with presence, device and identity-bearing sensor reports or change PlanSys2
state. It grounds \emph{any}, \emph{nearest} and station-slot policies against
current occupancy; payload selection requires five consecutive accepted
observations, after which the identity remains fixed.

\section{Step 3---build a fresh PDDL problem and plan}

Each cycle turns the grounded goal and latest valid \code{ObservedState} into a
fresh PDDL problem containing shuttle, location, occupancy, payload and device
facts. It receives no raw image or text; unknown, stale or conflicting state
blocks generation instead of implying free space. Transport actions require
validated side, location, occupancy and route facts. The direct, blocked-route,
normalisation and terminal cases are summarised in
\cref{tab:pddl-decision-rules}; the predicate-level reference remains in the
versioned PDDL artefact indexed by \cref{app:reproducibility}.

POPF minimises symbolic setup and relocation cost, not travel time. The executive
uses the PlanSys2 planner service rather than its action Executor, translates the
plan, and selects only its first supported checked step. PDDL effects remain
predictions: the next cycle always starts from the observed post-action state.

\section{Steps 4 and 5---execute one step and verify its effect}

The translator makes a sparse named \code{TranslatedPlanStep}; the supervisor
checks it before publishing a typed command. The executive then checks the
observed effect and replans if incomplete. Final-slot success requires the
DZI/controller evidence in \cref{chap:interfaces}; a command alone is never
success.

\paragraph{Why this record counts as success.}
The five rows are not five equivalent success claims. The confirmed request
fixes what must be achieved, and the current-state row shows why the returned
action was applicable; neither proves that motion occurred. Likewise, the
supervisor's accepted command proves authorisation, not arrival. The effect row
is the decisive evidence: two reports newer than the command identify R3 at the
requested slot, and the controller independently reports that the target slot
was reached and motion was \code{DISABLED}. Only after those declared
postconditions agree does the executive append
\code{task\_goal\_satisfied}. The trace therefore separates intended effect,
symbolic prediction, command authorisation and observed effect instead of
equating a planner response or published command with success.

\paragraph{Scope of the example.}
B03 deliberately uses one direct move so that every software boundary remains
visible in a short record. It does not exercise blocker relocation, route
restoration or competing occupancy; those cases appear in the twelve-case
matrix in \cref{sec:v4-closed-loop-campaign} and
\cref{tab:campaign-case-details}.

The implemented loop grounds, plans and authorises one action at a time, then
replans from the observed effect. The B03 trace shows this contract end to end;
the campaign in \cref{sec:v4-closed-loop-campaign} covers blocker clearance and
occupancy changes beyond that direct-move example.
