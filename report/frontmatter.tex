\chapter*{Abstract}
During this internship, I developed a unified Gazebo environment for the
industrial and mobile robotics platforms of the MFJA. Room~315 combines two
rail-transport cells, four industrial-robot workstations, cameras and
programmable devices, but these heterogeneous elements needed compatible
geometry, identities, interfaces and state descriptions before they could
support repeatable experiments. The objective was therefore not only to
reproduce the room visually, but to provide a coherent simulation for
multi-robot launch, shuttle routing, scene observation and task-level English
requests.

I reconstructed the MFJA third-floor geometry in Autodesk Inventor, designed
the furniture and static contents of Rooms~314 and~315, and exported the
resulting assets to Gazebo. In Room~315, I integrated the project-supplied
transport parts and attributed upstream robot models, then implemented
configuration-driven launch through Robot Operating System~2 (ROS~2), typed
interfaces and a calibrated directed transport network. The rail backend manages
stable shuttle identities, switches, stoppers, sensors, occupancy and temporary
blocker relocation. This created reproducible operating scenes while keeping the
different robot command interfaces explicit.

For task-level operation, I used learned models only to interpret uncertain
inputs: a local language model converts English requests into checked task
fields, and a paired-camera ResNet-18 estimator produces structured scene
observations. PlanSys2 uses Planning Domain Definition Language (PDDL) rules to
generate an inspectable symbolic action sequence. A software supervisor then
authorises one typed action at a time and checks its observed effect before
planning continues. This neuro-symbolic separation prevents either learned
model from directly commanding simulated equipment.

The frozen visual model achieved 99.94\% rail-segment classification accuracy
on labelled Final Test targets. Under the stricter joint criterion, 90.90\% had
both the correct segment and a longitudinal error no greater than 5\% of that
segment's length. The isolated closed-loop campaign completed 12/12 declared
tasks, and the five injected-fault scenarios passed 5/5 without a false success.

These results apply only to the evaluated Gazebo configuration, fixed synthetic
cameras, known shuttle fleet and kinematic rail model. They do not establish
transfer to physical cameras or machines, long-run reliability or
machinery-safety certification.

\textbf{Keywords:} ROS~2; Gazebo Harmonic; industrial robotics; digital twin;
multi-robot systems; flexible manufacturing; PDDL; PlanSys2; computer vision;
language-and-vision interface; neuro-symbolic control; software supervision.

\chapter*{Résumé}
Durant ce stage, j'ai développé, dans le simulateur robotique Gazebo, un
environnement commun pour les robots industriels et mobiles de la Maison de la
Formation Jacqueline Auriol (MFJA). La mission portait sur la salle~315, qui
comprend deux cellules de transport sur rails et quatre postes de robots
industriels. Le travail a combiné leur intégration via ROS~2, un système de
transport multi-navettes calibré, la perception par caméras et l'interprétation
de requêtes en anglais. Les modèles appris proposent des descriptions
structurées de la tâche et de la scène, mais seuls les contrôles déterministes,
la planification symbolique et la supervision autorisent les commandes. Cette
interface neuro-symbolique n'est donc pas une politique
vision--langage--action de bout en bout.

J'ai conçu sous Autodesk Inventor la géométrie du troisième étage, le mobilier
et les équipements statiques des deux salles détaillées, puis je les ai exportés
vers Gazebo. Pour la salle~315, j'ai créé son modèle dédié et assemblé ses postes
et cellules sur rails avec les éléments de transport fournis par le projet et
des modèles de robots externes cités.

Des lancements configurables et des interfaces ROS~2 typées relient les robots
simulés, la topologie des rails et le superviseur d'exécution. Le modèle local
Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct propose les champs structurés de la tâche ; un ResNet-18 à
deux vues estime, pour chaque navette connue présente, son segment, sa position
relative, sa boîte englobante et son état de chargement. Après validation,
PlanSys2 et son planificateur POPF produisent une séquence symbolique exécutée
action par action, avec vérification de chaque effet observé.

Le modèle visuel figé a atteint 99,94\,\% d'exactitude pour l'identification des
segments sur les cibles annotées du test final. Selon le critère conjoint plus
strict, 90,90\,\% des estimations associent le bon segment à une erreur
longitudinale inférieure ou égale à 5\,\% de sa longueur. L'évaluation en boucle
fermée dans Gazebo a réussi 12/12 scénarios positifs. La campagne d'injection de
fautes a réussi 5/5 scénarios, sans faux succès. Ces résultats concernent
exclusivement la configuration Gazebo évaluée et ne démontrent pas une aptitude
au déploiement sur les équipements physiques.

\textbf{Mots-clés :} ROS~2 ; Gazebo ; robotique industrielle ; jumeau
numérique ; systèmes multi-robots ; PDDL ; PlanSys2 ; vision ; commande
neuro-symbolique ; supervision logicielle.

\section*{Project-specific terms}
\begin{description}
  \item[Acceptance gate] A pass/fail criterion fixed before evaluation, distinct
  from a project-management decision milestone.
  \item[Checkpoint] Model weights saved after an epoch; freezing prevents further
  updates before evaluation.
  \item[Closed loop] Execution in which each command is followed by a new
  observation and a check of its expected effect before planning continues.
  \item[Consumed historical Test] The predecessor test partition that was
  evaluated once and is therefore no longer locked or used as the current Final
  Test.
  \item[Digital twin] A versioned digital representation of a defined physical
  system that includes its relevant information flows.
  \item[Fail-closed] A policy that blocks an operation when required state is
  missing, stale, contradictory or from an unapproved source.
  \item[Final Test] The preregistered post-freeze partition used as the
  project's final visual-model evidence.
  \item[Hard-case Validation] The current development partition used for
  checkpoint selection and calibration, distinct from the historical Grouped
  Validation and the Final Test.
  \item[Historical Grouped Validation] The predecessor validation partition
  that selected the checkpoint later used to initialise the V4 backbone; it is
  not a direct V4 input.
  \item[Neuro-symbolic interface] An architecture in which learned components
  propose fixed-schema task and scene fields, while deterministic validation,
  symbolic planning and the software execution supervisor determine which
  commands may be published.
  \item[\code{ObservedState}] A timestamped state snapshot whose accepted facts
  carry source, confidence and known/unknown/stale/conflicting status.
  \item[Post-selection regression set] A small development partition checked
  only after checkpoint selection. Retained artefacts call it the
  \emph{Development Canary} or use \code{canary} in paths and schema fields.
  \item[Public topology] The common logical route graph and its canonical
  segment labels exposed to ROS~2 and planning, distinct from each rail's
  internal geometric curve identifiers.
  \item[Selected Gazebo profile] A hash-identified Gazebo runtime that I selected
  after manually reviewing its retained evidence. Selection does not enable
  actuation; operator activation is separate.
  \item[SHA-256] A cryptographic digest used here to identify the exact files or
  model weights associated with an evaluation.
  \item[Shuttle] An independently controlled pallet carrier that travels around
  one of the two Room~315 rail cells and may be empty or carry a payload.
  \item[Software execution supervisor] The deterministic component that checks
  state/safety before publishing a typed command, distinct from the human
  supervisors and operator.
  \item[\code{TaskGoal}] The immutable structured task created after a
  fixed-schema text candidate passes rule validation and operator confirmation.
\end{description}
